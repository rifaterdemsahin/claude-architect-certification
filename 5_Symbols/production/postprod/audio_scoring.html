📖 Emotions & Music Scoring Guide ▼

1️⃣ First Principles — What Music Actually Is

Music is organised sound moving through time. Every emotional feeling a score produces comes from just four raw ingredients. Master these and you can score any sentence to feel exactly the way you want.

🎵
Pitch
How high/low a note is (frequency in Hz). Notes are named A–G. Pitch + pitch relationships = melody & harmony.
🥁
Rhythm
When notes happen — the pulse. Tempo is measured in BPM (beats per minute). Faster = energy.
🔊
Dynamics
How loud or soft. This is your intensity dial — sparse & quiet feels intimate; loud & full feels epic.
🌈
Tonality (Key)
The emotional colour of the music — set by the scale. The big switch: major vs minor (see next card).

💡 Emotion = Tonality (key) × Intensity (dynamics) × Tempo (BPM). Change any one and the feeling flips. Scoring is just choosing these three deliberately per sentence.

2️⃣ Major vs Minor — The Core Emotional Switch

A scale is a ladder of 7 notes inside one octave. Major and minor use the same 7 letters but place the small steps (half-steps) in different spots — and that tiny shift is what flips bright → dark.

Interval recipe (W = whole step = 2 semitones, H = half step = 1 semitone):

C Major CDEFGABC W-W-H-W-W-W-H → bright, resolved, happy, triumphant
C Minor CDE♭FGA♭B♭C W-H-W-W-H-W-W → dark, tense, serious, melancholic
Major notes Minor notes The 3 lowered notes (E♭, A♭, B♭) that make it minor Root (home note)

How to choose: positive, curious, optimistic, epic → MAJOR. Serious, cautionary, reflective, mysterious, sad → MINOR. When unsure, minor is the safer bet for tech/explainer content — it sounds authoritative without being cheesy.

3️⃣ How to Select & Score Music (5-Step Framework)

Name the emotionRead the sentence and write one feeling word in the Emotion field (e.g. “curious”, “cautionary”, “triumphant”).
Pick the keyMap the emotion to major or minor using the table below.
Set the intensityLow → 1–2 instruments, sparse (piano, soft pad). Medium → 3–5 (add pulse/bass). High → full ensemble, loud, driving.
Set the pace (BPM)Slow 60–80 (calm, reflective) · Normal 90–110 (steady explainer) · Fast 120–140 (energetic, urgent).
