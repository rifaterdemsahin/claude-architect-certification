The recording is the process. Everything else is set dressing.

One page to read before you sit down to record. It exists for a single reason: to turn the camera away from your competence and toward the viewer's outcome.

SC.01

Two loops, one reward

Building is a closed loop. You write the thing, it runs, you get an instant, unambiguous "you're good at this." No waiting, no audience, no doubt. Recording is an open loop: the first take is rough and the feedback is delayed and uncertain.

The same split shows up in the numbers. Likes are the closed loop — cheap, early, affirming. Retention is the open loop — the signal that actually tells you whether the thing works. You keep collecting the reward that feels good and under-weighting the one that's true.

The fix isn't motivation. It's moving the reward: make finishing the take the hit, not the take being good.

SC.02

Demonstration or transfer

Every video does one of two things. It demonstrates your competence — "here's my pipeline, here's everything I built" — or it transfers capability to the person watching. The set tour, or the scene.

Viewers retain when the video is about them getting something, not about you having done something. A builder records the way a builder thinks: comprehensive, sequential, complete. Completeness is exactly what kills retention.

The set tour

Context that mattered to you while making it. Config walkthroughs. The complete version. The viewer didn't come for this.

The scene

The specific thing they can do by the end. The payoff the title promised. The reason they pressed play.

SC.03

Whose fantasy

You don't reverse the audience's desire. You reverse which way the camera points.

Right now it points at your fantasy — "I am competent, look what I built." Production turns it 180° toward their fantasy — the version of themselves they're reaching for: "I can build like this, I can have this new kind of work."

Same footage. Same capability. Camera turned around. Your competence becomes the vehicle, never the subject.

SC.04

Pre-production has no bottom

The set gets built in pre-production — the research, the tooling, everything you understand in detail. And it's supposed to stay there. You build the set; you don't show it.

But the trap is real: pre-production has no floor. There's always more to research, more to wire up. If you tell yourself "I'll do the deep building now and reverse it for the audience later," then later is exactly where recording goes to die.

So the audience's outcome governs the whole pipeline, not just production. Build the smallest set that makes the scene work — not the most complete one you're capable of. The completeness instinct doesn't get a free pass just because it moved upstream.

SC.05

The instrument

Likes feel nice; they can't tell you where it broke. The audience-retention graph can. The shape is the diagnosis — go read it before you guess.

SC.06

The forcing function

The fix is never another improvement. It's a constraint.

One take. One module. A date on the calendar. Published near-unedited. The reward comes from done, not from perfect. The thing fighting you is the belief that the process has to get better before the recording is good enough. It doesn't. The recording is the process.

Before you hit record

five questions · answer them, then roll
  • Does the first minute pay the promise the title made — or is it setup?
  • Is the camera on their outcome, or on my capability?
  • Is this the smallest set that works, or the most complete one I could build?
  • Is there a date on this module — or just an intention?
  • Am I about to polish, or about to ship?
